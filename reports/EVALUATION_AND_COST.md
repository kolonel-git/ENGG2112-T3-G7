## Evaluation design

We fitted models on 2016, 2017, 2018, 2020, selected model settings and alert thresholds on 2021, 2022, and reserved 2023, 2024, 2025, 2026 for the frozen evaluation (2026 is partial through April). The time split avoids mixing neighbouring days across training and evaluation. Labels purge rows whose 60-day horizon crosses a split boundary. Train-only preprocessing and healthy-reference fitting follow the shared model contract. Every day with features receives a score for plotting; metrics include only labelled turbine-days, excluding in-episode, exclusion-zone, purged, censored and poorly observed days. An episode is evaluable only if a labelled scored day exists in its warning window.

The official budget is **1 false alarm per turbine-month**. An alert occurs when the score is strictly greater than its threshold; a false alarm is an alert on a negative labelled day. Exposure is labelled turbine-days divided by 30.4375, including positive and negative days. It is not full calendar fleet exposure. The validation threshold allows at most the floor of budget times exposure false-alert days. Tied scores remain together and may leave budget unused. Test uses that threshold unchanged, so its realised rate can exceed the allowed validation budget and must still be reported.

PR-AUC is computed as average precision over labelled days, with prevalence as the constant-score reference. Episode recall is the share of evaluable episodes with at least one alert 1 to 30 days before the start. Median lead time uses the earliest such alert for each detected episode and excludes missed episodes. One alert may cover overlapping episode windows; consecutive alerts are not consolidated into visits. There is no cooldown or one-to-one matching rule.

Intervals use 1000 turbine bootstrap resamples with replacement, a 95% percentile interval and seed 42. Entire turbine histories are resampled because adjacent feature and label windows overlap. Thresholds stay fixed across bootstrap draws. These intervals describe turbine variability conditional on the fitted model and selected threshold; they do not include model selection uncertainty or establish transfer to another wind farm.

## Alert burden and missed episodes

The validation sweep holds scores and model settings fixed and recalibrates the threshold separately at each configured budget. F27 plots false-alert days against missed evaluable episodes for both targets and both fleet variants; diamonds mark the official budget. The companion analysis includes all models and the random floor at every budget. The following reference-model check retains turbine 2304515 in the primary fleet and excludes it in the sensitivity run.

| Target | Fleet | Model | False-alert days | Total labelled alert days | Missed / evaluable | Recall | Random recall |
|---|---|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| A | all | R1_zscore | 318 | 981 | 87 / 209 | 0.584 | 0.780 |
| A | excl_2304515 | R1_zscore | 309 | 929 | 82 / 195 | 0.579 | 0.774 |
| B | all | R1_zscore | 290 | 950 | 66 / 192 | 0.656 | 0.760 |
| B | excl_2304515 | R1_zscore | 281 | 899 | 61 / 177 | 0.655 | 0.746 |

False-alert days approximate review burden; missed episodes approximate the coverage lost when alerts are restricted. Total labelled alert days include alerts on positive days too. Alerts on unlabelled days are scored for plots but excluded from this burden estimate, so it is not the complete workload of an always-on deployment. Repeated alerts can refer to the same episode. Neither alert days nor missed inferred episodes can be equated with maintenance visits, prevented failures or lost energy. The dataset provides no verified visit cost, failure cost or intervention benefit, so we do not estimate currency savings or choose an economic optimum. The official budget stays fixed.

The budget sweep is descriptive validation analysis. Settings and thresholds were selected using this same period, so differences across sampled budgets are not held-out performance or significance tests. The turbine-exclusion result is a sensitivity check, not a reason to discard a difficult turbine from the headline fleet. Sensor gaps, outage months, drift and unverified fault-time semantics limit the interpretation of both targets.
